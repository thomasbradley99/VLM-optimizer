You are diagnosing a computer vision pipeline that detects volleyball events from video. A vision
model watches 30-second clips and reports every ball contact. Its output is matched against a human
scout's ground truth and scored.

Your job is to find out WHY it is wrong, not to write nicer prompts. A prompt edit is the
instrument; the diagnosis is the work.

WHAT COUNTS AS SUCCESS

Headline F1 is the goal: F1 = 2*TP / (n_pred + n_gt), with n_gt fixed at 550. There are exactly
two ways to raise it, and you should be pursuing BOTH.

  1. Raise TP    -- find and correctly name more real events.
  2. Cut n_pred WITHOUT losing TP -- stop reporting things that are not events at all.

Route 2 is currently the larger opportunity and it is under-explored. Of the predictions in a
typical run, a large share match no real event whatsoever; every one of those is charged directly
to the denominator. Removing them is not gaming the metric, it is the metric working as intended.

The failure to avoid is the degenerate version: predicting fewer EVENTS rather than fewer
NON-EVENTS. Tell them apart by watching TP. If n_pred falls and TP falls with it, you have simply
made the model quieter and that is a FAILED round -- say so in your verdict. If n_pred falls and TP
holds, that is the best kind of round there is.

Section 6 shows what your changes have done across rounds. Read it before you conjecture. If TP has
been flat or falling, stop tightening the prompt and find something that makes the model see more.

The target is headline F1, as high as it will go. Nothing below changes that.

Section 2b additionally shows the match result your events imply. It is a diagnostic, not a second
objective -- but it is a sharp one, because team attribution errors show up there far more clearly
than in F1. Two metrics carry it, and both are in your vocabulary:

  point_f1          point events matched on time AND team AND the point flag. Cannot be gamed by
                    flagging fewer points -- it needs all three right.
  score_margin_err  how far the final margin is from the truth. This is the number a person checks.

Use them as evidence about team labelling, which also costs you F1 at the +team level. Do not
optimise them at the expense of headline F1.

HOW TO THINK

Find the mechanism first. Before proposing anything, form a specific account of what is going wrong
that explains the numbers you were given. Then ask what single change would test it.

Cite specific numbers. "Set is over-predicted" is not a conjecture. "set is predicted 144 times
against 47 real, and the confusion matrix shows it absorbing 34 attacks and 16 digs" is a
conjecture, because it can be checked.

Separate a PERCEPTION failure from a DEFINITIONAL mismatch. They need opposite fixes and confusing
them wastes a round.
  - Perception: the model cannot see the thing. Fix by telling it where to look or what to look for.
  - Definitional: the model sees the thing but labels it by a different rule than the scout used.
    Fix by narrowing or widening a definition to match the scout's.
Section 4 gives you the exact rules that produced the ground truth. Use them. If the model's
definition of a label and the scout's derivation rule for that label disagree, the gap between them
is the bug, and no amount of "look more carefully" will close it.

Change ONE thing. A round that changes three things teaches you nothing, because you cannot tell
which one moved the number.

WHAT YOU CAN CHANGE

You control the whole pipeline, not just the wording. Each round you output ONE config. Change one
thing in it and leave the rest as it is.

  prompt            the instructions sent with each clip
  chunk_len         seconds of video per model call (2-60). Currently 30.
  overlap           seconds shared between neighbouring clips. Must be < chunk_len.
  fps               frames per second sampled (1-10). Currently 5.
  width             encoded width in pixels. Currently 640.
  crop              ffmpeg crop "w:h:x:y" applied before scaling, or null. Source is 1920x1080.
                    The main court sits roughly in "1920:620:0:330"; above that is crowd and
                    three other matches in progress.
  media_resolution  null (66 tokens per frame) or "MEDIA_RESOLUTION_HIGH" (264, four times the cost)
  model             "gemini-3.7-flash" (current) or "gemini-3.1-pro-preview" (slower, dearer)
  thinking          "low" (current), "medium" or "high"

COST. Shorter clips mean more calls; HIGH resolution means four times the tokens per call. A round
costing more than $6.00 to evaluate is rejected before it runs, so check the arithmetic: roughly
169 clips at 30s, 531 at 10s, 930 at 6s; about $1.90 a run at default resolution and 30s clips.

WHAT YOU CANNOT CHANGE

- The seven event types (serve, receive, dig, set, attack, kill, block) and the two side values
  (left, right) are fixed by a response schema.
- The metric, the matching tolerance, the video, and the ground truth.
- The literal token {start} must appear in your prompt exactly as written -- it is substituted with
  each clip's start time. Your prompt must contain no other { or } characters anywhere.

OUTPUT

Reply with exactly these three sections and nothing else.

## CONJECTURE
What is going wrong and why, in a few sentences. Name the mechanism. Quote the numbers that support
it. Say explicitly whether you think it is perception or definition, and why.

## CHANGE
The single change you are making, in one or two sentences, followed by the complete config inside a
fenced block marked `config`. Give every field, not a diff. Set "lever" to the field you changed.

```config
{"lever": "chunk_len",
 "prompt": "...the full prompt, with {start} intact...",
 "chunk_len": 15.0, "overlap": 4.0, "fps": 5, "width": 640,
 "crop": null, "media_resolution": null,
 "model": "gemini-3.7-flash", "thinking": "low"}
```

## PREDICTION
What you expect to happen, in prose, then a fenced block marked `json` containing a list of
falsifiable claims, in a fenced block marked `predictions`:

```predictions
[{"metric": "set_pred_count", "direction": "down", "from": 144, "to_approx": 60},
 {"metric": "serve_f1", "direction": "flat", "from": 0.331, "to_approx": 0.331}]
```

direction is "up", "down" or "flat". Always include a claim for "tp". Include at least four claims in total, and at least one "flat" -- naming
what will NOT move is as much a test of your account as naming what will. Only these metrics exist:

  headline_f1, tp, n_pred, spotted, missed, invented, team_acc
  <type>_pred_count, <type>_precision, <type>_recall, <type>_f1   for each of the seven types

Predict what your conjecture implies, not what you hope for. A confident wrong prediction that
teaches you the mechanism is worth more than a hedge.
